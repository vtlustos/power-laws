Observed everywhere in nature

Fat tails

  • Extreme values are far more common than the normal distribution predicts.
  • They are part of the data, not outliers.

At x = 10: normal (μ = 0, σ = 1) says 10−23, power law (α = 3, xmin = 1) says 1 %.

A bit of history

Who discovered them

1896

Vilfredo Pareto: wealth

The Italian economist noticed that 20 % of people owned 80 % of the land, and the same pattern held in tax records of other countries. This gave us the 80/20 rule.

1949

George K. Zipf: words and cities

The Harvard linguist found that the 2nd most common word appears half as often as the 1st, the 3rd a third as often… The same holds for city sizes.

The weird part

No average, no deviation

  • The mean and standard deviation can be infinite.
  • Power law mean is finite only for α > 2, variance only for α > 3.
normal (μ = 3, σ = 1)finite mean (α = 2.5, xmin = 1)infinite mean (α = 1.8, xmin = 1.25)
Normal vs. power law

No characteristic value

Normal

$$p(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\, e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^2}$$

Depends on the distance from the mean, in units of σ.

Power law

$$p(x) = C\,x^{-\alpha}, \quad x \ge x_{\min}$$

No mean, no σ. Just x to a constant power α, above a threshold xmin.

normal (μ = 1, σ = 2)power law (α = 3, xmin = 1)

Power law = straight line on a log-log plot, with slope −α.
